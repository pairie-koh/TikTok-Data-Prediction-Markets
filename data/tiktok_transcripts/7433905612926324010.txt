Betting markets show that former President Donald Trump has a slight edge over Vice President Kamala Harris in the election. The question is, should we trust them? Betting markets generally have been a good predictor of US elections, but there have been exceptions. In 2016, some betting markets gave Hillary Clinton a more than 80% chance of beating Trump in the days before the election. Trump won an upset victory this election. The latest polls show Trump and Harris in a dead heat, and the accuracy of betting markets has become a politically loaded question. Harris supporters argue that the markets are vulnerable to manipulation, skewed to a right leaning user base, and distorted by a handful of huge bets. Trump supporters say the markets are reacting to polls tightening in his favor and signs of Harris slowing down. Today, betting markets say they're a more accurate predictor of election results than polls. Not only did most pollsters get the election wrong in 2016, but they're facing growing difficulties gathering information as fewer Americans use landline phones and many don't answer calls from strangers.
