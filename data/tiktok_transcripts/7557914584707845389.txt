Billionaires are piling into prediction markets because they believe it could be the next big thing. Charles Schwab, Henry Kravis, Jung Jae and even Coinbase Brian Armstrong are all invested in prediction market platforms like Kalshi. These platforms basically turn any event into a tradable asset. Instead of buying shares of companies, you're trading contracts tied to questions like will inflation be above 3%? Will Taylor Swift show up at the Super Bowl? And what's interesting is that prediction markets actually often outperform polls and expert forecasts. That's because they're able to aggregate thousands of beliefs into one number, which is the price. And that number becomes a kind of collective intelligence engine. Brian Armstrong goes even further to say that one day prediction markets could rival the New York Times as a source of trusted information. And we know that information democratizes access because historically, only hedge funds or big corporations had any mechanism to hedge against world events. Now anyone can participate in this new financial primitive, which is exciting enough that Charles Schwab, the man who literally built one of the world's largest brokerages was so convinced that he walked into Kalshi's office is met the 27 year old founders and within minutes decided to invest. And in just the last three months, Kalshi has searched to command over 62% of global prediction market trading volume.
