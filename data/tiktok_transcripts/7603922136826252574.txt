Prediction markets aren't gambling, and here's why. When I bet on sports books like drafting and Fanduel, I'm betting against the house. They set the odds, they take the other side, and they charge a vig or fee on every trade. Currency markets are very different. When I buy a 65 cent contract that the next Fed rate will have 25 basis points cut, I'm not buying it from Kalshi. I'm buying it from another trader who thinks the outcome of that will be no. Kalshi is just like the New York Stock Exchange. They're just matching* a buyer with the seller and executing that contract for us. Um, this is the same reason Kalshi is CFTC Regulated as a derivatives exchange. It's honestly pretty similar to when CME Traders go and buy oil or, uh, corn futures. Uh, they're just both derivative markets. Instead of corn, you're just trading Fed rate cuts. Both are peer to peer, both are observable outcomes, and both are derivative markets. There's no bookie taking a vig, just traders on opposite sides of the contract.
