When I was really young, I wanted to be a carpenter, but straight away I realised there was not much glamour in it. So my second choice was hairdressing. I ended up working for three of the busiest salons in the 70s, and sadly all three of them went bankrupt from being top of the game. And the reason why they all went bankrupt is because they didn't look after the team. I'm so proud that we managed to keep our staff for so long. It's just by looking after them. That's a lesson I learned when I worked for other people, that if you don't look after your team, you really haven't got anything. Because it's very easy to open a salon and make it look sexy, but at the end of the day, people will come to the hairdressers. I didn't really envisage the business growing to the size it is now, and my goal at the time was just to pay the mortgage, because I borrowed so much money. I really wanted to be different, so I spent about nine months looking for church gothic furniture. It was very unusual in those days. I had this thing in my head that the day you open your salon, everybody's going to walk in, and it didn't happen. So I used to sit outside on the pavement and wonder if anybody's going to walk in. So that was quite scary. I always wanted to feel that whoever was sitting in the chair was the most important person in the salon. That was our motto, and it made me realize that if you're actually giving people what they want, they will find you. And within 18 months, it just went crazy. you
