I'm Ukrainian and here's what 12 years of Russian war taught me about avoiding propaganda. In the first part I talked about how to avoid being lied to in times of war. And now let's talk about the media. I know a lot of people think this is where propaganda mostly comes from and that's partially true. But if you really don't want your suspiciousness to push you into even more propaganda, you need to see the difference between these two types of media. So there is what I called editorially run media and there is state run media. And yes, neither of them is objective. But they are not objective on very different levels. So the first type is your mainstream media. They are mostly privately owned, sometimes publicly funded. But what they have in common is that even though they are independent, they all have an agenda. They report real news, they don't distort the fact, but they frame them in a way that serves their narrative. So they're not lying to you, but they're making you see exactly the angle they want you to see. Now the second type is state run media. This is something completely different. They exist to serve the government, an authoritarian government. Which means they would do anything, lie straight to your face, to make their government look good and their enemies look bad. Of course, on the regular day-to-day news they would tell you the truth, but when it matters to the government, you're getting pure propaganda from the state. So you need to remember the difference. With editorially run media, you need to learn how not to get influenced by their narrative. With state run media, just don't trust them.
