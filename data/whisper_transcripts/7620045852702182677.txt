As the South Australian election is beginning to wrap up, there's still a few seats left to be decided, but I keep seeing this one question ravaging debate all over the internet. And that is, has this election actually been a win for One Nation? Because on the surface, you'd probably say no. Labour have won in an absolute landslide, taking 32 of the 38 decided seats so far, and currently 38% of the first preference vote. And One Nation will not be forming government. But when you look at the numbers and the influence, it quickly becomes obvious that anybody who immediately said no in this question didn't really look at this election properly, or through the lens of One Nation. More specifically, what they were looking to do when this election first began. One Nation polled at about 22% in the lower house in this election, and nearly 24% in the upper house. That puts them ahead of the Liberals who have collapsed to about 19%. And here's the key point to consider. This did not come out of nowhere. In the weeks leading up to the election, many state-specific polls had One Nation in that exact range. Some, a bit ludicrous, as high as 28-27%, but most of them were either 20, 21, 22, or 23. About exactly where they sat in both the lower and upper house. So this wasn't necessarily a protest spike or a one-off. It confirms exactly what One Nation supporters and the One Nation Party themselves would have been looking to confirm coming into this election. And that was, people who showed up for polls would show up for an election. So no, this isn't necessarily a win in the traditional sense. But when you go from the fringe to outperforming a major party, reaffirming your polling results, and actually winning a seat with a few still pending, that's not just a good result, but it's 100% a win for One Nation.
