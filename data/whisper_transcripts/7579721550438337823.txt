Republicans have won the battle in a Tennessee congressional district, but Democrats still have the edge in the overall war for control of the House of Representatives. Last night, Matt Van Eps, an Army combat veteran endorsed by President Donald Trump, defeated Democratic State Representative Afton Bean by about nine percentage points. However, this was still a major Democratic overperformance, this time in a constituency that favored Donald Trump by 22 points in the 2024 election. We found that this year so far, Democrats have outrun Kamala Harris's 2024 election performance by an average of 17 percentage points in five special elections. Democrats also swept off your elections held last month in Virginia, New Jersey, and other states. All of this points to 2026 acting more like a typical midterm election, a progress report on the sitting president and the governing party. Just two presidents since the end of World War II have led their party to House seat gains in midterm elections, Bill Clinton in 1998 and George W. Bush in 2002, when both presidents had approval ratings above 60 percent. Donald Trump's approval rating is closer to 40 percent. The Tennessee result also called attention to how Democrats could put in play some districts they didn't seriously contest in 2024 and force Republicans to spend millions of dollars to defend districts that might otherwise win easily.
