What do you think this means to finally have, in a century, the first legal election prediction market? What kind of impact do you think that's going to have on the election? It actually enhances democracy in a way. There's a lot of research that says that when people are putting money behind their predictions, they actually do more research, it increases voter engagement, it does all of these things. And you also get a single number, a better forecast for the election, because people are putting money behind their positions. It's very easy to say, I think this candidate's going to win, but when you're actually backing it with real money, you get better forecasts. What it means is, I think, two things. People can now participate and engage a bit more meaningfully with the election. People have had this sort of feel of, like, I don't have control of who wins, and I'm just going to take the impact and the consequence, no matter what it is. And now you can actually do something about it. You can make money off of it if you have a conviction about who is going to win, or you can hedge against an outcome that is negative for you, which I think is a very powerful thing. The second thing, and I think this is a big topic of conversation right now, is that these markets are a mechanism for truth. There's been a lot of misinformation in the last 10 years, and it's rampant. It's actually getting worse and worse. Polls are getting less and less accurate. It's actually the best mechanism to get more truth about who's actually going to win. We're letting the market speak instead of pundits, pollsters. Markets don't lie. Just ask people to put money where their mouth is, have some skin in the game, and when they do, it becomes unbiased, it becomes more truthful, and I think that's our approach to basically delivering more truth to the world.
