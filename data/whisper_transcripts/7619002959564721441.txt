A journalist reports about a missile strike. That's why he is now threatened with murder. On March 10th, an Iranian missile strikes near Beit Shemesh in Israel. There are no injured, no casualties and in the local media it is only treated very briefly. The journalist Emanuel Fabian from the news portal Times of Israel also reports about the struck missile. Thereupon, messages follow from people who urge Fabian to correct his speech. They write that the Iranian missile is only a remnant of a captured missile, so not a strike. But Fabian remains steadfast and refers to the information that comes from the Israeli military itself. So the article remains unchanged. And the news is getting more and more blatant. Fabian gets hidden threats, anonymous calls and even threatening letters on various social media platforms. Because people bet on the platform PoliMarket, namely on the war. Whether it was a strike or a captured missile decides the outcome of their bet. A total of around 15 million dollars have several thousand users questioned whether Israel will be the target of a successful Iranian attack on March 10th. The strike at Beit Shemesh probably decides which party wins.
