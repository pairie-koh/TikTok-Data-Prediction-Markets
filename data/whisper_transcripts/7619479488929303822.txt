The president is labeling, gilding, and remaking as much of Washington, D.C. in his image as he can possibly get away with. The Kennedy Center is now the Donald J. Trump Kennedy Center. The Oval Office is covered in ornamental gold. The East Wing is now a construction pit making way for a gargantuan Mar-a-Lago-esque ballroom that will dwarf the White House itself. There are plans for an arc to Trump across the river from the Lincoln Memorial. And now, there is going to be a gold coin with his face on it. Yesterday, the U.S. Commission of Fine Arts, a panel made entirely of Trump appointees, approved a 24-carat commemorative gold coin featuring President Trump. You can see it right here with him, both of his fists pressed on the resolute desk under the word liberty. The coin is a collectible and meant to mark the nation's 250th birthday this July 4th. It is not intended to circulate as currency. But there is a separate $1 Trump coin in the works, one that does raise legal questions as federal law bars living presidents from appearing on U.S. currency. Still, the Treasury is pressing ahead with markups on the government's U.S. Mint site. A bipartisan advisory panel created by Congress has refused to support either coin, warning the designs clash with the country's founding principles, calling them, quote, abhorrent to the Declaration of Independence. We don't have kings here. As Ari Fleischer pleaded this morning regarding the gold coin, honors like this are bestowed after someone has served, especially now, he says, this would be a huge mistake that would hurt the president and his cause.
