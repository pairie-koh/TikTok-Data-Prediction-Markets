This is the simple math behind why March Madness is so special. On the left side of this page, the blue bar represents the 896,000 kids that played high school basketball in the United States last year. From that group, around 36,000 go on to play in the NCAA, meaning just 4% make it to the next level, while the rest stop somewhere along the way here in gray. Even that number, though, gets smaller as we zoom in. Of those 36,000, 10,600 are Division 1 athletes, with the rest spread across Division 2 and Division 3. But we're not done yet. You see, March Madness is made up of 68 Division 1 teams for both men and women, about 2,000 total players coming out of this group here. That means just 0.23% of high schoolers will make it to the big dance, and beyond that, there are only 60 NBA draft picks and 36 WNBA draft picks every year. What I'm trying to get at here is that for a majority of these 2,000 players who've made it this far, these two weeks are the true pinnacle of their basketball careers which have been building for more than a decade. For seniors, it's often the last time they'll ever play competitive basketball. No next season. No next level. Which is why every game has a serious weight to it. Every upset. Every buzzer beater. Every mistake. Because for many on these courts, this is the end of the line. For a special few, it's just the beginning, and a possibility to reach the highest mountaintops in the sport.
