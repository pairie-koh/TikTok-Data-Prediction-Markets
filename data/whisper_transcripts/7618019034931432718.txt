There are two paths that we could go from here with the conflict in Iran when it comes to the real estate market where we're at sitting right now in March. Scenario one, conflict gets resolved sooner rather than later. Strikes wrap up, oil stabilizes, and the Strait of Hormuz stays open for business. In this scenario, inflation cools off, bond yields come back down, and our interest rates go back down with it. And right back to where we were seeing that 5.98% just a couple weeks ago, the spring season gets back on track with a little bit of a delay. Path number two is this conflict does not get resolved quickly. Iran keeps disrupting oil supply through the Strait of Hormuz, which carries typically 20 million barrels of oil a day. And with that, oil stays elevated, inflation doesn't cool down, bonds stay up, and Fed can't cut rates because of inflation being high. In that scenario, interest rates stay above 6%, maybe even pushing higher, spring inventory could sit longer, more price reductions start showing up, and those sellers who were counting on a hot spring or those buyers who thought they'd be able to afford a certain price point have to rethink their strategies. And depending on how fast this conflict can get resolved, we'll have a direct correlation on where we sit in the housing market going forward over the next couple weeks and months.
