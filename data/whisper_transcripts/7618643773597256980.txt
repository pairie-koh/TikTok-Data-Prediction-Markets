Here we have to talk about a core concept, which is called a predictor. What is a predictor? Simply put, a predictor is a bridge used by the blockchain world to obtain real-world information. Because the smart contract itself cannot directly know what happened in the real world, it must pass the predictor and feed the data under the chain to the chain, such as price, weather, competition results, and even news reports. In the prediction market, the predictor determines the final settlement result. The problem is here. If the data source of the predictor is affected, or even manipulated artificially, the result is no longer fair. In this incident, the reporter actually played the role of a human-made predictor. His report is the data source, and the players involved did not hire hackers to hack the system, nor did they attack the contract, but directly affected the data source itself by threatening, using, and changing the content of the report, thereby affecting the market settlement. This is essentially a new kind of predictor attack, which is more terrible than traditional hackers. Because what it attacks is humanity, not code. Many people have always believed that the predicted market of PolyMarket is completely fair, relying on the group's wisdom. But this thing tells you that as long as there is enough interest, someone will try to manipulate the data source. Once the data of the predictor is not reliable, the result of the whole market will be false, and even manipulated artificially. What's more terrible is that this model will affect the real world.
