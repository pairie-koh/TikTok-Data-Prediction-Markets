Would you have put money on the ousting of Iran's supreme leader? That's what thousands of bettors have done, and they could get a big payout for it since the assassination of Ayatollah Khamenei. But it's stirring up questions about ethics and insider trading. Prediction markets allow bettors in the US to make trades on real-world events that have a yes or no outcome. They bet against each other, and depending on the outcome, you can win big. Supporters claim prediction markets are becoming tools that reflect the wisdom of the crowd that are being used to anticipate events from the weather in New York to huge geopolitical activity. But questions are being raised as to whether we should be betting on worldwide conflicts, and are insiders with prior knowledge cashing in? Bets on geopolitics on the popular platform PolyMarket jumped to record highs in the week the US strikes hit Iran, hitting over $400 million in bets placed. And analytics firm Bubble Maps claims six suspected insiders made $1.2 million by betting on a US strike on Iran, and claimed most had bet in the hours before the strike happened, and specifically bet that it would happen by the 28th of February, raising suspicions that bettors had prior knowledge. The authority that regulates this activity in the United States bans trading on assassination, terrorism or war, but PolyMarket are an offshore business and not subject to the rules. We reached out to PolyMarket for comments. Now lawmakers in the US are claiming it's insane, and that these markets should be illegal.
