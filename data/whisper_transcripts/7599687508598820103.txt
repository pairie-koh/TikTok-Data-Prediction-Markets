Lately, publications of candidates and other political actors are appearing, showing results of Polymarket as if they were public opinion polls. And here we have a very serious problem. Polymarket is not a pollster. Polymarket does not apply statistical methodologies, does not work with representative samples, does not segment by region, age, socioeconomic level, etc., etc. Polymarket is a betting platform, a prediction market where people bet money on future events, like who wins an election, if there will be a war, how much will the dollar cost, or who will be the next president of a country. Thus, what Polymarket shows is not a public opinion study, it is betting money, it is not an electoral preference. Let's look at the case of the presidential elections in Bolivia. This is what Polymarket showed a day before the elections. According to this betting platform, Rodrigo Paz did not exist electorally, he was irrelevant to the bettors, but that was not the reality. Because Rodrigo Paz went to the second round and won those elections, and today he is president of Bolivia. That is why we cannot use a betting platform as if it were a survey, because one thing is public opinion, and another thing is money betting futures. And its use is more of a narrative strategy, and every narrative strategy has a goal, build perception, create legitimacy, create a majority feeling, and condition a political behavior. As we see, Polymarket is a betting market, but the market does not vote. People do. I'm Carlos Helfer and this was Polidatos de Cubo. See you on social media.
