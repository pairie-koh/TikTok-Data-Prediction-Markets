Prediction markets aren't gambling, and here's why. When I bet on sportsbooks like DraftKing and FanDuel, I'm betting against the house. They set the odds, they take the other side, and they charge a VIG or fee on every trade. Prediction markets are very different. When I buy a $0.65 contract, the next Fed rate will have a 25 basis points cut. I'm not buying it from Cauchy. I'm buying it from another trader who thinks the outcome of that will be no. Cauchy's just like the New York Stock Exchange. They're just buying a buyer with a seller and executing that contract for us. This is the same reason Cauchy is CFTC regulated as a derivatives exchange. It's honestly pretty similar to when CME traders go and buy oil or corn futures. They're just both derivative markets. Instead of corn, you're just trading Fed rate cuts. Both are peer-to-peer, both are observable outcomes, and both are derivative markets. There's no bookie taking a VIG, just traders on opposite sides of the contract.
