America's largest corporations have a Calshi and a Polymarket problem. For example, if I'm an intern working in Google's Corporate Communications Department, the company's about to post quarterly earnings that are worse than expected, and I know this because I've seen the press release. Nobody else does. I could, in theory, go on Polymarket or Calshi and post a bet about which direction the stocks can go in, or if the company's simply gonna beat on earnings or miss on earnings. That's the stuff that reporters like me love to get their hands on, but which we're not allowed to trade on for very good reason. That's insider trading. Semaphore reached out to more than 100 companies, law firms, and banks to figure out if they had rules about how employees use information on prediction markets. By and large, we didn't hear back, suggesting that companies are kind of flat-footed about this new era of insider trading. There are some companies, notably, that do have policies around prediction markets. OpenAI, for example, told us that their corporate policies prohibit employees from using insider information on any platform, including prediction markets like Polymarket and Calshi. If you think about junior bankers, junior lawyers, even junior flacks who sit at PR firms, they have access to a ton of information on upcoming deals, on earnings, on corporate scandals, and right now, dozens of firms we reached out to have no policy or no comment on what those policies are, posing a big problem, not just for companies themselves, but the kinds of people they choose to do business with.
