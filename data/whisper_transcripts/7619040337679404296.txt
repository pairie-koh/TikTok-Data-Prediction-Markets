Warren Buffett is predicting 10 years of no returns in the stock market. And the last time he made a similar prediction was in 1999. And his prediction ended up being right on the money. So in 1999, Warren Buffett issued a memo to his investors. And he cautioned that it's very hard to come up with a persuasive case that equities will over the next 17 years perform anything like they did over the past 17. And his reasoning was based off two major factors that are resurfacing in today's economy. Number one is high interest rates, which act as anchors to stocks. And with oil prices going up and inflation expected to rise, we can expect interest rates to stay higher for longer. And the next factor, which is the more important one, is valuation. So in 1999, the S&P 500 peaked at 44 times earnings. And just last week, we peaked at 40 times earnings. And since that call out in 1999, it took the stock market 13 years to break its previous all-time highs. But we disagree with Warren Buffett on one thing. Because even though the index does look expensive on the surface, many stocks are trading at great value. And that includes companies like Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, and even Nvidia.
