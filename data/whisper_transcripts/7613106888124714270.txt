What really happens when geopolitics becomes a betting market? The Financial Times reports that the U.S. attack on Iran was preceded by unusually large well-timed bets, 12 suspicious accounts, $330,000 in profit, placed in the days before the airstrikes, on Polly Market. About half the bets came in the six hours before the attack. That's not just a market story, it's a psychology story. When anonymous wallets suddenly bet big and win perfectly, three things happen. One, pattern detection kicks in. Perfect timing feels like insider knowledge, at least to me, whether proven or not. Trust erodes. If military action becomes tradable, people start questioning how surprising it really was. Number three, war becomes financialized. Conflict turns into a speculative instrument. Polly Market runs on crypto. Anonymous wallets, public blockchain trails. If insiders ever start trading on military events, prediction markets become more than just curiosity. They become a national issue. The real question isn't just legality, it's this. What really happens when geopolitics becomes a betting market?
