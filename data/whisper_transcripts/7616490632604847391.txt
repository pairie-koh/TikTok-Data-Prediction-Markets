Polymarket just got banned in the Netherlands. Before that, it was banned in Portugal and Hungary. And regulators in these countries are calling it what they believe it really is, which is illegal gambling. Now, whether you agree with that or not, we need to look at why this is happening. The Dutch Gambling Authority ordered Polymarket to immediately stop operating, threatening fines of €420,000 per week if it doesn't comply, and their reasoning was very clear. Users are staking money on uncertain real-world outcomes such as elections, political legislation, and even court ruling. The prediction markets themselves are trying to defend themselves in court by calling them event contracts, but the regulators are arguing that even if you call them that, the economic behavior looks like betting. And in many countries, betting on political events is simply illegal. Prediction markets have processed over $13 billion in monthly volume and tens of millions of transactions. Just for the Super Bowl alone, prediction markets hit a record $1.63 billion. That means serious money is involved, but now combine that with insider knowledge. We already see politicians in the United States accused of trading stocks with privileged information. We've seen crypto markets move around coordinated political announcements by presidential administrations. So what happens when an insider can bet on an election, what they will say during a speech, or even legislation that they directly influence? This creates a powerful incentive structure, and the danger isn't just financial. If a law, a court ruling, or an election outcome can be financially gamed by insiders, the consequences affect society, not just portfolios, and that is why some regulators in some countries are stepping in. They are prioritizing what they call systemic integrity over innovation. Now here is my honest view. I understand why countries are banning these platforms, but I do not believe that the United States will do the same. Not because the system is pure or they want free market capitalization or whatever, but because prediction markets align with the culture of financialization and constant stimulation. History has shown that crumbling societies often lean into distraction, and this whole situation reminds me of this quote, give them bread and circuses and they will never revolve. This Roman phrase described how leaders kept citizens occupied with food and entertainment during the decline of the Roman Empire. And now, in the United States and many other countries around the world, we are living in a time of rising costs of living, home unaffordability, and constant digital gambling. Does this not sound like bread and circuses? And that is exactly why I am still bullish on prediction markets, especially within the United States, because I simply do not believe that our governments and regulators give a damn about us and only want to keep us distracted.
