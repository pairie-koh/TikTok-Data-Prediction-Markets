California just had a sheriff seize more than half a million ballots from an election office. That sheriff is Riverside County Sheriff Chad Bianco, and he is also running for governor. Bianco says he is investigating a ballot count discrepancy from a November 2025 special election on redistricting. That measure passed by more than 80,000 votes, and he says he wants the ballots physically counted and compared to the reported total. But election officials say his claim does not hold up. Bianco says the discrepancy was about 45,800 votes. County officials say the real difference was only about 100. And they say the handwritten intake logs he is relying on were kept by temporary workers and were never meant to be the official count. California Attorney General Rob Bonta called the seizure unprecedented and warned it could damage trust in elections. Nearly 1,000 boxes of ballots and election materials were taken with a warrant, and now the recount is set to continue under a court-appointed special master. But the bigger issue here is not just the ballots. Critics say this is what election distrust looks like in real time, take shaky numbers, create a public spectacle, and turn it into political fuel during a governor's race.
