In prediction markets, if one trader thinks that there's an 80% chance of something happening and another trader thinks that there's a 60% chance of something happening, obviously the price is going to be priced at 70 cents on the dollar, right? Wrong. If we make the assumption that both traders have the same bankroll and that they're both betting Kelly, which is considered rational, we can see that the equilibrium price actually diverges pretty significantly from the expected mean price. We can derive this from the Kelly formula by setting the amount of money spent by each trader to be equal. This is the derivation, the result is at the bottom. P1 is the fair value that is assigned to this event by trader 1 and P2 is the fair value assigned by trader 2. P1 is greater than P2. X is the trade value or the price that these trades will happen at. If we take this formula and plug in the 60% and 80% from the previous example, we see that we get 75% or 75 cents on the dollar rather than the expected 70 cents that you would get from just having the mean of the problem. This is an interesting result because it shows that even if both traders are completely rational, the price of the contract can diverge from the intuitive mean probability and more towards the extremes. This is because prediction markets, like every financial market, has market participants considering not only EV, but also the risk that they take on.
