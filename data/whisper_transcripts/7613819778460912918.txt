Do you know who to vote for in the Paris municipal elections? And above all, what do each candidate propose concretely? Municipal elections are the elections that shape your daily life. Housing, transport, school, security, ecology. And since everyone doesn't have time to read the programs, here is a clear description. In Paris, we vote by round, for a list. And it is then the elected representatives who designate the or the mayor of Paris. The dates are set on March 15 and 22, 2026. To vote, you have to be registered on the electoral lists. In France, you are automatically registered on the lists at 18 years old or during a naturalization. Otherwise, you have to do the process yourself. To find out if you are registered, simply look at the government website. The Paris municipal elections of 2026 oppose several major figures. Emmanuelle Grégoire, Union de la Gauche or LFI. Sophia Chikirou, from LFI. Rachida Dati, LR, Modem, UDI. Pierre-Yves Bournazel, Renaissance Horizon. Sarah Knafo, Reconquête. And Thierry Mariani, from the National Assembly. Paris is facing a high housing crisis. Grégoire proposes a very voluntary strategy. 60,000 houses created and 200,000 renovated to improve energy efficiency and fight against precariousness. Chikirou wants to strengthen direct housing aid for 50,000 tenants, APL and ALS, and strengthen energy renovation. Dati defends a more patrimonial line. 10,000 social housing per year and a more controlled urban transformation. Bournazel focuses on a rebalancing between private and public, with a simplification of building permits. Knafo and Mariani focus on the so-called abuses and a more restrictive management of social housing. Then, regarding mobility, what place does the car have? A survey by Moving On shows that 8 out of 10 French people support the measures of sustainable mobility. Bicycles, peaceful areas and reinforced transport. Grégoire and Chikirou want to continue the reduction of the car, develop public transport and soft mobility. Dati and the far right denounce an asphyxiation of motorists and promise to restore fluidity. Bournazel, on the other hand, seeks a balance between appeasement and pragmatism. Regarding security, Dati, Knafo and Mariani want more armed municipal police and video surveillance. Grégoire and Chikirou favor prevention and the fight against the structural causes of violence. Bournazel proposes moderate reinforcement and better coordination between municipal police and national police. And finally, regarding ecology and the framework of life, Grégoire pursues the Hidalgo line. Vegetation, fight against heat waves, and emission reduction. Chikirou goes further with the gratuity of some essential public services such as the gratuity of school canteens or some transport for young people. Dati defends a so-called pragmatic ecology, less restrictive for motorists. Bournazel focuses on innovation and energy efficiency. And the far right relays the climate emergency to the background. So, go to your general culture book and I challenge you to dig deeper the municipal programs of each of your candidates according to your city.
