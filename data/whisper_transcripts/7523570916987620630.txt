It was the morning of September 11th, 2001. No one could have predicted what was about to unfold when the first plane struck the North Tower of the World Trade Center. Many believed it was a tragic accident, but in a nearby building, inside a fast-paced trading office, one man stood up and said, ''The market is going to crash.'' His colleague asked, ''How do you know?'' ''It's a terrorist attack,'' he replied. ''Bet against the market!'' Within seconds, the entire office was alive with urgency. Traders picked up their phones, calling investors across the globe, urging them to short the market, an action that just minutes earlier would have seemed reckless or absurd. But then, the second plane hit. At that moment, the truth became undeniable. It was an organized terrorist attack. Panic spread. The stock market began to nosedive. The man had been right. As the financial world turned red, their positions surged. They had shorted the market at the perfect time. Although no exact figure is confirmed, it is believed that the profits exceeded $15 billion. In the midst of one of the darkest days in American history, where thousands died and millions were affected, someone saw it coming. And in doing so, they made billions. Cold, calculated, and terrifyingly accurate.
