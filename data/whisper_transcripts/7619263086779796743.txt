Almost every political pundit is predicting Labor in a landslide, some even predicting the Liberals will be left with two or three seats. My view treads a well-worn political path that nothing is as good or as bad as it seems. Both major parties see One Nation as the looming threat, with opinion polls putting them ahead of the Libs on a primary vote. Why couldn't people see it coming? I could. I've been speaking about this for a long time. I'm alive to the fact that there is restlessness in some parts of the community. My thoughts differ from many. I'm tipping One Nation will have a major influence with preference voting, but it won't be enough to win a lower house seat. I'm forecasting Labor finishes with 32 seats, the Liberals languishing on 11, with four independents making up the 47. Both leaders must be running on empty by now, but they're refusing to show it. You never give up on the underdog. What an underdog she is, according to a Seven News viewer election poll on who will be the next Premier. Hearns rated a paltry 26.8%, with Peter Malinowskis on 73.2%. We'll be pouncing on the first votes counted in several perilous Liberal seats, including Moriarty, Hartley, Bragg, Unley, Morford and Colton. Any substantial swing to Labor in these seats, it's almost game, set and match. We'll have them live from 6.30 tomorrow night.
