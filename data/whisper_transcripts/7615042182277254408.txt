A few days ago, a betting website says that Rafael López Aliaga will be the president of Peru, which has a trend of more than 40%. But there is an uncomfortable question that no one is telling that sector. Polymarket is not a survey, it is not Ipso, it is not Datu, it is not CPI, it is not a survey even of a university. It is a platform of bets, of interest, political interest in this case. There people put money to bet who will win in the next elections in Peru or in any other part of the world. We have a second before reaching a conclusion. Who in Peru uses Polymarket? The farmer of Ayacucho? The taxi driver of Pucallpa? The merchant of Gamarra? The butcher of Carne y Caquetá? No. We have the traders, the analysts, the people of the crypto world, the sectors of the high digital process, investors, etc. It is a very small elite of the country, probably from Lima and some other parts. In democracy there is a simple rule. The vote of a Miró-Quesada is exactly the vote of a Quispe of Curahuasi. Those who decide the elections do not live in betting platforms, they live in the sectors C, D and E. And there the presidencies are defined. But there is something that has caught my attention, the appearance of López Chao. That could indicate that certain progressive sectors of Lima are already looking for a new political reference. And the betting market sometimes detects the movement of elites before the surveys themselves. So be careful with selling certainties with Polymarket. Polymarket does not decide elections. The elections in Peru are not betted, they are won on the street. And the street is not yet, at this moment, with Polymarket. It is still for that high undecided vote to define why it will vote.
