President Trump has a pattern of not expressing sympathy for his perceived political enemies, even in the face of death or political violence. I hate my opponent, and I don't want the best for them. That was the case when President Trump responded to the death of former FBI Director Robert Mueller. This was his words on True Social. Robert Mueller just died. Good. I'm glad he's dead. He can no longer hurt innocent people. It is typically customary for U.S. presidents to offer condolences after the deaths of prominent leaders and public servants, regardless of their political party. It's a tradition that President Trump has not followed. Last year, when Hollywood director Rob Reiner, a frequent critic of the president, was brutally stabbed to death, the president actually blamed him for his own death. Robert Mueller served in the FBI for more than a decade, including leading the agency during the 9-11 attacks. The president actually interviewed him during his first term in 2017, and just one day later, he was named special counsel over an investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election. President Trump repeatedly called that investigation a witch hunt, but Mueller notably never publicly confronted the president about his personal attacks against him.
